## English Invitation

Subject: Public RFC: test Action-Effect's mapping and permission boundaries

We invite concrete technical review of Action-Effect, an MIT-licensed interface
draft from Ond Holdings Inc., Toronto, Ontario, Canada. It exchanges coupled
effect descriptions and explicit unknowns without treating evidence as permission.
No OSuite account, product or implementation is required.

Our same-project GitHub-comment study compared a description/checker path with
a complete structured OPA adapter under shared policy and dispatch controls.
Both matched 108/108 native lane instances and 52/52 eligible decisions. The
controlled live run matched 32 planned checks and created 12 comments, including
four read-only recoveries after deliberately dropped successful acknowledgments.
The protocol's accepted-input offline median cost was 3.97% higher; this is not
a speed or safety-superiority claim. Downstream notifications/webhooks remain
unknown, and ID-less recovery is not execution attribution.

Can you propose a minimal synthetic counterexample for tuple loss, context
binding, unknown-versus-failure handling, policy freshness, or ambiguous delivery?
Please separate expected effects from expected authorization, identify the
profile/version, and compare against a complete adapter rather than a lossy
straw baseline. Independently maintained adapters and fair OPA/Cedar comparisons
would be useful.

Protected evaluation needs independently authored inputs and outcomes, actual
custody and access controls before execution. Propose scope publicly, not hidden
inputs or answers. Tooling exists; external evaluations remain zero.

## Company LinkedIn Draft

Ond Holdings Inc. invites technical review of Action-Effect: exchanging supported
effect descriptions without confusing evidence, current permission, and observed
completion.

A bounded same-project GitHub-comment study found exact mapping and authorization
parity with a complete structured OPA baseline. The real-provider run matched
32 planned checks with 12 new comments, including four controlled lost-ACK
recoveries without resend. Denials, review, invalid-policy failures and duplicate
suppression remain separate outcomes, not 32 successful creation tasks.

The protocol's accepted-input offline median cost was 3.97% higher. Tiny,
non-isolated live timing samples support no speed ranking. Notifications and
webhooks remain outside complete observation; ID-less recovery establishes new
current content, not causal execution attribution. No external evaluation occurred.

We seek concrete counterexamples, independently maintained adapters, and scoped
evaluation proposals with separate input/expected-outcome custody. Test coupled
permissions, configuration drift, policy freshness and failure semantics.
Baseline parity and negative findings are welcome.

The repositories retain MIT and require no OSuite service. This is public review
of an experimental interface and working paper, not customer deployment,
certification, or standards-body approval. Please keep protected cases and
expected outcomes out of public issues.
